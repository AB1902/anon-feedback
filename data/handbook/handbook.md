# Nimbus Technologies Pvt. Ltd. — Employee Handbook

> **Fictional document.** Nimbus Technologies is an invented company. This handbook was drafted for an academic project to serve as the retrieval (RAG) corpus of an anonymous feedback system. It is not legal advice and does not describe any real organisation's policies.

Version 3.2 · Effective 1 April 2026 · Owner: People & Culture (HR)

---

## 1. Introduction and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose
This handbook sets out the policies, entitlements and expectations that apply to everyone who works at Nimbus Technologies Pvt. Ltd. ("Nimbus", "the Company"). It explains what employees can expect from the Company and what the Company expects from employees.

### 1.2 Who this applies to
These policies apply to all full-time and part-time employees, interns and fixed-term staff across all Nimbus offices (Pune, Bengaluru, Mumbai, Gurugram and Hyderabad) and to employees working remotely. Contractors and vendor staff must follow Sections 2, 3, 10 and 11 while on Company premises or systems.

### 1.3 Precedence
Where this handbook conflicts with an employee's appointment letter, the appointment letter prevails. Where either conflicts with applicable law, the law prevails.

### 1.4 Updates
HR reviews this handbook every year. Material changes are announced by email at least 15 days before they take effect, except where a change is required by law.

## 2. Code of Conduct

### 2.1 Core principles
Every employee is expected to act with integrity, treat colleagues with respect, protect Company and customer information, and comply with the law.

### 2.2 Respectful behaviour
Employees must communicate professionally in person, in meetings, on calls and in writing. The following are not acceptable at any level of seniority:
- shouting at, insulting, mocking or publicly humiliating a colleague;
- threats, intimidation or aggressive gestures;
- repeated, unjustified criticism intended to undermine a person;
- excluding a colleague from work activities as a form of punishment.

A single serious incident or a pattern of minor incidents may each amount to misconduct.

### 2.3 Responsibilities of people managers
Managers are held to a higher standard. In addition to Section 2.2, managers must:
- give feedback privately and constructively, focusing on work, not the person;
- set realistic goals and priorities and review workload with their team at least monthly;
- apply policies consistently and avoid favouritism in allocation of work, leave approvals, ratings and promotions;
- never discourage an employee from raising a concern or using the grievance or feedback channels.

### 2.4 Conflicts of interest
Employees must disclose to HR and their manager any personal, financial or family relationship that could influence their decisions at work, including relationships with vendors, customers, candidates or direct reports. Undisclosed conflicts are treated as misconduct.

### 2.5 Gifts and hospitality
Employees may not accept gifts, cash, or hospitality worth more than ₹2,000 from any vendor, customer or partner. Anything above this limit must be declined or reported to the Ethics Committee within 7 days.

### 2.6 Consequences of breach
Breaches of this Code are handled under the Disciplinary Procedure (Section 9.6). Outcomes range from counselling and written warnings to termination, depending on severity and history.

## 3. Respectful Workplace and Prevention of Sexual Harassment (POSH)

### 3.1 Policy statement
Nimbus has zero tolerance for sexual harassment. This policy is framed in line with the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 ("POSH Act"). As a matter of Company policy, Nimbus extends the protections of this section to employees of all genders.

### 3.2 What counts as sexual harassment
Sexual harassment includes any unwelcome act or behaviour, directly or by implication, such as:
- physical contact and advances;
- a demand or request for sexual favours;
- sexually coloured remarks, jokes or messages;
- showing pornography or sexually explicit material;
- any other unwelcome physical, verbal or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature.

It also covers implied or explicit promises of preferential treatment, threats of detrimental treatment, interference with work, or creating an intimidating or hostile work environment in connection with such conduct. The "workplace" includes offices, client sites, Company travel, offsites, Company-provided transport and online channels such as chat, email and video calls.

### 3.3 Internal Committee (IC)
Nimbus has constituted an Internal Committee at each office. Each IC is presided over by a senior woman employee, includes at least two employee members, and includes one external member from an organisation committed to women's causes or familiar with sexual harassment issues. At least half the members are women. Current IC member details are published on the intranet.

### 3.4 Filing a complaint
A complaint may be made in writing to any IC member or to the confidential address posh-committee@nimbustech.example. Complaints should be made within three months of the incident (or the last incident, in a series). The IC may extend this by a further three months where there were reasons that prevented an earlier complaint. Complaints raised through the anonymous feedback system are routed directly to the IC (see Section 12.4); the IC will use the ticket ID to invite the complainant to proceed formally if they wish.

### 3.5 Inquiry process
The IC may first attempt conciliation at the complainant's request; no monetary settlement can be a basis of conciliation. Otherwise, the IC conducts an inquiry following the principles of natural justice and completes it within 90 days. During the inquiry, the IC may recommend interim relief such as transferring either party, changing reporting lines or granting leave to the complainant.

### 3.6 Confidentiality and protection
The identity of the complainant, respondent and witnesses, and the details of the inquiry, are kept confidential. Retaliation against anyone who raises a complaint or participates in an inquiry is a serious offence under Section 12.6.

### 3.7 Bullying and discrimination
Bullying, ragging, and discrimination based on gender, caste, religion, region, language, disability, age, sexual orientation, marital status or pregnancy are prohibited. Such complaints that are not sexual in nature are handled under the Grievance Redressal Procedure (Section 9) and, where serious, by the Ethics Committee.

## 4. Leave Policy

### 4.1 Leave year
The leave year runs from 1 January to 31 December. Leave is credited monthly and pro-rated for employees who join mid-year.

### 4.2 Earned / privilege leave
Employees receive 18 days of earned leave per year (1.5 days per month). Up to 30 days may be carried forward to the next year. Earned leave should be applied for at least 7 days in advance for absences of 3 days or more. Managers must respond to leave requests within 3 working days and may decline or reschedule only for documented business reasons; leave may not be refused repeatedly without HR review.

### 4.3 Sick leave
Employees receive 12 days of sick leave per year, which does not carry forward. A medical certificate is required for sick leave longer than 3 consecutive days.

### 4.4 Casual leave
Employees receive 6 days of casual leave per year for personal matters, usable in half-day units.

### 4.5 Maternity leave
Eligible employees are entitled to 26 weeks of paid maternity leave for the first two children, in line with the Maternity Benefit Act, 1961, and 12 weeks thereafter. Adoptive and commissioning mothers are entitled to 12 weeks. After returning, employees may request a phased return or work from home where the role allows.

### 4.6 Paternity and partner leave
Employees are entitled to 15 working days of paid paternity/partner leave, to be taken within six months of the birth or adoption.

### 4.7 Bereavement leave
Up to 5 days of paid leave is available on the death of an immediate family member.

### 4.8 Public holidays
Nimbus publishes a list of 10 fixed and 2 optional holidays for each location before the start of the year.

### 4.9 Leave without pay
Employees who have exhausted their leave may request up to 30 days of leave without pay with HR approval.

## 5. Working Hours, Overtime and Right to Disconnect

### 5.1 Standard hours
The standard working week is 40 hours, normally Monday to Friday, with core collaboration hours from 11:00 to 16:00 local time.

### 5.2 Meetings outside core hours
Recurring meetings should be scheduled within core hours. Calls before 09:00 or after 20:00 should be limited to genuine emergencies, production incidents or unavoidable time-zone overlap, and should not be routine.

### 5.3 Right to disconnect
Outside working hours, on weekends and during leave, employees are not expected to read or respond to messages unless they are on an assigned on-call rota. Not responding outside working hours must not be held against an employee in performance reviews.

### 5.4 On-call and release work
Teams that require on-call coverage must publish a fair rotation at least two weeks in advance. Employees receive an on-call allowance of ₹1,500 per weekend day on call, and one compensatory day off for every weekend day or public holiday actually worked.

### 5.5 Overtime and workload concerns
Sustained work beyond 48 hours per week for more than two consecutive weeks must be flagged by the manager to the HR Business Partner, who will review staffing and priorities with the department head. Employees who believe their workload is consistently unmanageable should raise it with their manager or HRBP, or through the anonymous feedback system.

## 6. Hybrid Work and Work From Home (WFH)

### 6.1 Hybrid model
Nimbus follows a hybrid model. Most roles are expected in the office 2 days per week on team-agreed anchor days. Some roles (facilities, logistics, lab and front-desk roles) are fully on-site.

### 6.2 Additional WFH
Employees may request additional WFH days from their manager. Requests should not be unreasonably refused; managers must apply the same criteria to all team members.

### 6.3 Equipment and allowance
Nimbus provides a laptop and, on request, a monitor and headset. Employees on hybrid or remote arrangements receive an internet and utilities allowance of ₹1,000 per month.

### 6.4 Security when working remotely
Employees must use the Company VPN, lock their screens when away, and avoid working on confidential material in public places.

### 6.5 Late-evening travel
Employees who leave the office after 20:00 are entitled to Company-arranged cab transport. Women employees travelling after 20:00 will have a security escort or a verified driver with live trip tracking, and the Company will confirm their safe arrival.

## 7. Compensation and Benefits

### 7.1 Pay principles
Nimbus aims to pay fairly and competitively. Pay is based on role, level, skills and performance, and never on gender or any other protected characteristic. Salary bands for each level are published internally.

### 7.2 Payroll
Salaries are credited by the last working day of each month. If a salary payment is delayed for reasons attributable to the Company, HR will communicate the reason and expected date within 2 working days.

### 7.3 Annual increments
Increments are decided through the annual performance cycle and take effect from 1 April. Increment letters are issued by 30 April. Employees may request an explanation of their increment from their manager and HRBP.

### 7.4 Variable pay and sales incentives
Variable pay for non-sales roles is paid annually based on Company and individual performance. Sales incentives are calculated monthly against targets agreed in writing at the start of each quarter, and are paid no later than 45 days after the end of the month in which they are earned. Targets may not be changed retrospectively. Disputes about incentive calculations may be raised with Sales Operations and escalated to Finance if unresolved within 15 days.

### 7.5 Pay equity review
HR conducts an annual pay equity review across gender and comparable roles. Employees who believe they are paid inconsistently with peers in the same role and level may request a confidential review.

### 7.6 Benefits
Benefits include group medical insurance of ₹5,00,000 per family per year (employee, spouse, children and two parents or parents-in-law), term life insurance, accident insurance, an annual health check-up, and access to a free and confidential Employee Assistance Programme (EAP) offering counselling.

### 7.7 Reimbursements
Business expenses must be submitted with receipts within 30 days and are reimbursed within 15 days of approval.

## 8. Performance, Learning and Career Growth

### 8.1 Performance cycle
Goals are set in April, reviewed mid-year in October, and assessed in March. Every employee must receive written goals within 30 days of the start of the cycle.

### 8.2 Ratings and calibration
Ratings are calibrated across teams by department heads and HR to ensure consistency. Employees are entitled to see the reasons for their rating.

### 8.3 Rating appeals
An employee who disagrees with their rating may file an appeal with their HRBP within 15 days of receiving it. The appeal is reviewed by a panel that excludes the employee's direct manager, and a decision is communicated within 30 days.

### 8.4 One-on-ones
Managers should hold a one-on-one with each direct report at least every two weeks to discuss priorities, feedback and development.

### 8.5 Learning budget
Each employee has an annual learning budget of ₹25,000 for courses, certifications and conferences relevant to their role or career path, subject to manager approval. Employees also receive 5 paid learning days per year.

### 8.6 Promotions
Promotions are considered twice a year, in April and October. Criteria for each level are published. Managers must share with an employee, on request, what is needed to reach the next level.

### 8.7 Internal mobility
Employees who have spent at least 12 months in their current role may apply for internal openings. Managers may not block an internal move but may agree a transition period of up to 60 days.

## 9. Grievance Redressal Procedure

### 9.1 Purpose
This procedure provides a fair, timely way to resolve work-related concerns, such as workload, treatment by a manager or colleague, application of policy, pay or working conditions.

### 9.2 Step 1 — Informal resolution
Employees are encouraged, where comfortable, to raise concerns first with their manager. If the concern is about the manager, the employee may go directly to Step 2.

### 9.3 Step 2 — Formal grievance to HR
A formal grievance can be submitted to the HRBP in writing or through the anonymous feedback system. HR will acknowledge it within 2 working days and aim to resolve it within 15 working days.

### 9.4 Step 3 — Grievance Committee
If unresolved, the grievance is reviewed by the Grievance Committee (department head of another department, a senior HR representative and an employee representative). The Committee issues a written decision within 30 days.

### 9.5 Complaints about HR
Grievances about the conduct of HR staff, or about how HR handled a previous complaint, are reviewed by the Senior Review Panel, comprising the Chief Operating Officer, the Head of Legal and one independent director. HR staff named in such a complaint are excluded from its handling.

### 9.6 Disciplinary procedure
Where a grievance reveals possible misconduct, a fair investigation is conducted. The person concerned is informed of the allegation and given an opportunity to respond before any decision is made.

### 9.7 Records
Grievance records are kept confidential and accessible only to those handling the case.

## 10. Ethics, Fraud and Whistleblower Policy

### 10.1 Scope
This policy covers fraud, bribery, kickbacks, falsification of records or expense claims, misuse of Company assets, data manipulation, breaches of law, and serious breaches of this handbook.

### 10.2 Ethics Committee
The Ethics Committee comprises the Head of Legal, the Chief Financial Officer and an independent director. It reports to the Audit Committee of the Board and is independent of line management.

### 10.3 How to report
Concerns can be raised at ethics@nimbustech.example, via the ethics hotline, or anonymously through the feedback system, which routes fraud and ethics concerns directly to the Ethics Committee. Reports should be made in good faith and include as much factual detail as possible (what happened, when, and where evidence may be found), without needing to identify the reporter.

### 10.4 Investigation
The Committee acknowledges reports within 3 working days, decides within 10 working days whether to investigate, and aims to complete investigations within 60 days.

### 10.5 Protection
Whistleblowers acting in good faith are protected from retaliation even if the concern turns out to be unfounded. Knowingly false reports may result in disciplinary action.

## 11. Health, Safety and Wellbeing

### 11.1 Safe workplace
Nimbus maintains safe offices, warehouses and sites in compliance with applicable fire, electrical and building safety norms. Fire drills are conducted at least twice a year at every location.

### 11.2 Reporting hazards and incidents
Any employee who sees an unsafe condition (blocked fire exits, exposed wiring, unsafe machinery, missing protective equipment, unsafe transport) must report it immediately to Facilities or through the feedback system. Safety reports are routed to the Ethics Committee and the site Safety Officer. Any employee may stop work they reasonably believe to be unsafe without penalty.

### 11.3 Protective equipment
Employees in warehouse and logistics roles are provided with protective equipment free of charge. Work requiring protective equipment must not proceed without it.

### 11.4 Mental health
Employees can access free, confidential counselling through the EAP. Managers are trained to recognise signs of burnout and to direct employees to support. Employees may take sick leave for mental health reasons on the same basis as physical illness.

### 11.5 Ergonomics
On request, Facilities will provide an ergonomic assessment and suitable chairs, footrests or monitor stands.

## 12. Anonymous Feedback and Non-Retaliation

### 12.1 Purpose
Nimbus provides an anonymous feedback system so that employees at every level can raise concerns or suggestions without fear. Feedback is used to improve policies, management practices and working conditions.

### 12.2 How anonymity is protected
- Submissions are not linked to a login, name, email or device. The system does not store IP addresses.
- Each submission receives a random ticket ID, known only to the submitter, which they can use to read HR responses and reply.
- Submissions are automatically scrubbed of names, IDs and other identifying details and rewritten in neutral language before any reviewer sees them.
- Timestamps are stored only at the level of the calendar week.
- Group-level reports are shown only when at least 5 responses exist for that group. For senior leadership, responses are grouped at a broader level.

### 12.3 Who sees what
- People managers see aggregated summaries for their team only, never individual notes, and only when the minimum group size is met.
- Department heads see department-level trends and team comparisons.
- HR reviews individual anonymised notes to respond and act.
- The leadership team sees company-wide patterns only.

### 12.4 Special routing
Concerns about sexual harassment are routed directly to the Internal Committee (Section 3). Concerns about safety, fraud or ethics are routed to the Ethics Committee (Sections 10 and 11). Concerns about HR are routed to the Senior Review Panel (Section 9.5). These cases are not visible to the employee's manager or the general HR team.

### 12.5 Response commitments
HR acknowledges each submission through its ticket within 5 working days, and posts an update on the action taken or the reason no action is possible within 30 days.

### 12.6 Non-retaliation
No employee may be penalised, threatened, isolated or treated unfavourably for raising a concern, giving feedback, or participating in an inquiry. Attempting to identify the author of anonymous feedback is itself a breach of this policy and will be treated as serious misconduct.

### 12.7 Acceptable use
The feedback system must not be used to post abusive content, threats, or deliberately false allegations. Such submissions may be rejected automatically.

## 13. Data Privacy and Information Security

### 13.1 Employee data
Nimbus processes employee personal data only for legitimate employment purposes, in accordance with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. Employees may request access to and correction of their personal data held by HR.

### 13.2 Confidential information
Customer data, source code, financial information and unpublished plans are confidential and must not be shared outside authorised channels.

### 13.3 Acceptable use of systems
Company systems may be monitored for security purposes. Monitoring is never used to identify authors of anonymous feedback.

### 13.4 Reporting incidents
Suspected data breaches or security incidents must be reported to security@nimbustech.example immediately.

## 14. Facilities and Workplace Services

### 14.1 Office facilities
Each office provides clean washrooms, drinking water, a cafeteria or meal vouchers, a first-aid room and a quiet room. Facility issues can be logged with the Facilities helpdesk, which aims to resolve routine requests within 3 working days.

### 14.2 IT support
IT support is available 09:00–21:00 on working days. Laptop replacements for faulty hardware are provided within 5 working days.

### 14.3 Accessibility
Nimbus provides reasonable accommodations for employees with disabilities, including accessible workstations, assistive software and flexible arrangements.

## 15. Separation

### 15.1 Notice period
The notice period is 60 days for employees at manager level and above and 30 days for other employees, unless otherwise stated in the appointment letter. Unused earned leave may be adjusted against the notice period with manager approval.

### 15.2 Full and final settlement
Full and final settlement, including unpaid salary, leave encashment and reimbursements, is completed within 45 days of the last working day. Relieving and experience letters are issued on the last working day where clearance is complete.

### 15.3 Exit interviews
Departing employees are invited to an exit interview with HR, or may choose to share exit feedback anonymously through the feedback system.
